## Auto-Crop: Retained FOV (%)

| Angle (deg) | Old (1+tan) | Uniform Scaling | Off-Centre Crop |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | 84.6 | 36.9 | 59.8 |
| 10 | 72.3 | 3.1 | 27.7 |
| 15 | 62.2 | 7.3 | 7.4 |
| 20 | 53.8 | 48.4 | 0.1 |
| 25 | 46.5 | 115.3 | 44.4 |

The uniform scaling formula becomes numerically unstable for large off-centre offsets (the valid rectangle is no longer centred). The off-centre crop retains more FOV than uniform scaling for moderate angles (5-15 deg) but crops aggressively at 20 deg because the inscribed rectangle is narrow. The old `1+tan` rule retains the most FOV but leaves border voids.

**Note:** Roll is limited to ±5 deg in the UI to avoid the need for rotated-quadrilateral inscribed-rectangle computation.

## Pass 1→2 Attribution

The speedup from pass 1 to pass 2 comes from two changes: (1) replacing lexsort with single-key argsort via quantization, and (2) replacing the all-points 3x3 splat with adaptive fill-only splatting (about 9x fewer candidates). Both changes contribute.

## Remaining Risks

1. **Occlusion holes at large angles:** At yaw 25 deg, 23.8% of pixels are filled by the pyramid. These regions contain no real image data and show blocky artifacts (gradient ratio 0.49).
2. **Auto-crop FOV loss:** The off-centre crop retains only 7.4% FOV at 15 deg yaw. The `1+tan` rule retains 62.2% but leaves border voids. A hybrid approach (crop what you can, fill the rest) would retain more content.
3. **Single-image depth limitation:** Depth Anything V2 produces relative disparity, not metric depth. The mapping `z_near=1.0, z_far=4.0` is a tuning choice; different scenes may benefit from different ranges, but this is exposed as a UI slider.
4. **Roll over-crop:** Roll is limited to ±5 deg because the axis-aligned inscribed rectangle of a rotated quadrilateral shrinks rapidly.
